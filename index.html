Sovereign is a free geopolitical strategy game that runs entirely in your browser. Take any of 204 countries, set its taxes, welfare, industrial and monetary policy, and watch a month-by-month simulation respond — through growth, inflation, unemployment, elections and the patience of the public.

Fiscal policy

Income-tax brackets or a flat rate, corporate and land taxes, health and education, social protection, infrastructure, defence, housing, childcare and pensions — each with a visible budget cost and a lag before it bites.

The central bank

Set an inflation target and decide how independent the bank is. It runs its own reaction function; the more independence you grant, the less you control the rate and the faster expectations anchor.

Cities

Every country has real named cities. Fund local policing, prevention, services and maintenance, watch a crime-pressure index respond, and queue hospitals, metros, universities and freight terminals.

Trade

Sign five-year commodity contracts in food, energy, manufactures and technology. Deliveries are capped by real production capacity, tariffs become customs revenue, and sanctions or war suspend the route.

Politics

Eight parties, a hundred seats, coalitions and elections. Reforms cost political capital, unpopular ones cost approval, and a governing coalition has to be held together.

Diplomacy & defence

Embassies, research and climate pacts, non-aggression and defence treaties, sanctions. Split a defence budget across army, navy and air forces, and choose a doctrine.

The simulation is a real model, not a slider that goes up

Output is produced, not granted. Every month the game solves a small macroeconomic model for all 204 countries.

Supply — what the country can produce

A Cobb-Douglas production function, Y = A · K0.33 · L0.67. Investment builds a capital stock net of depreciation. Productivity grows with research, competition, openness and administrative quality — and catches up toward the frontier only where institutions, schooling and trade support it, so weak states converge slowly or not at all.

Demand — what it actually produces

An output gap driven by real interest rates against a modelled neutral rate, the fiscal impulse, competitiveness, trade and shocks. Demand moves output around the supply path. It cannot raise it for long — stimulus buys a boom and pays for it in inflation.

Money — the price level

A Taylor-type policy rule, a modelled neutral rate, a central-bank balance sheet and a credibility score. Inflation follows an expectations-augmented Phillips curve. A credible bank disinflates cheaply; a bank that keeps missing its target does not.

People — the labour market

Unemployment follows Okun's law around a structural floor set by labour rules, minimum wages, training and placement programmes. Participation responds to the pension age and childcare. Population changes through modelled fertility, ageing and migration.

An honest note on realism. These are recognisable textbook relationships, simplified to one line each and tuned for playability. Nothing here is estimated from data or calibrated to any real country, and no number the game shows — a growth rate, a crime index, a city budget — is a statistic about a real place. It is a game that argues with you in the language of economics, not a forecasting model.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. No price, no account, no advertising, no analytics and no tracking. Sovereign is one HTML file served as a static page.

Does it work offline?

Yes. Once the page has loaded, the entire simulation runs on your own machine. Nothing is sent anywhere. You can save the page to disk and open it later with no connection at all.

Where do my saved games go?

Into your own browser's storage, on your device. Browser storage is tied to the page address, so use Export save file in Sandbox if you want a portable backup or plan to play from a different location.

Does it run on a phone?

It does, and the layout adapts, but the interface has a lot of panels and is considerably more comfortable on a tablet or a laptop.

Are the country statistics real?

Starting conditions are rough, hand-set scenario values in the right order of magnitude, not current official statistics. City names are real places; the crime, budget and development figures attached to them are invented gameplay indices. The map's borders are a scenario simplification and are not a statement about sovereignty or recognition.

Can I play a country that isn't on the list?

Every UN member state, both observer states and nine further scenario entities are playable — 204 in total. Select any of them on the map and choose Play as.
